Meet the three

In a small AI startup, one person often does all three badly. In a large one, they're three separate people who need each other constantly. Here's how to spot each in the wild — the text below changes with the depth you picked above.

Product Manager

Owns the "what & why"

Field marks

  • Talks in user problems, not model architecture
  • Has a metric they check every Monday
  • Can explain why a feature was cut, not just what shipped
"What's the user problem we're actually solving here — not the coolest model we could ship?"

Plate III

Where each role goes wrong

Every role has a signature way of failing. Recognizing it early is half the job of fixing it.

Product Manager

  • Feature-chasing: ships whatever's trendy (a chatbot, an agent) with no metric proving it helps users.
  • No opinion on "no": can't say why something was cut, only that it was.
  • Vanity metrics: celebrates model accuracy going up while user complaints go up too.

Program Manager

  • Status theater: the deck says "green" the week before everything turns out to be blocked.
  • Late escalation: only surfaces a risk after it's already caused a slip.
  • Process for its own sake: adds meetings and trackers that no team actually needed.

Delivery Manager

  • Over-committing: says yes to every ask, then the whole sprint slips at once.
  • Busy, not done: the board looks full of activity but nothing actually ships.
  • Blocker-blind: the same task sits "in progress" for a week before anyone asks why.
